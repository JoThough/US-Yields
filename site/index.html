How to read this chart

Each point is the Treasury's official par yield for a maturity, calculated at the end of the trading day. Use the arrows (or the [ and ] keys while the chart is focused), the date box or the slider to move through history. Pin curve keeps the current curve on the chart so you can compare dates; remove a pin with the × in the legend.

“RRP” is the award rate of the Fed's overnight reverse repurchase facility. The shaded band is the FOMC's federal funds target range (a single target before December 2008). TIPS real yields start in 2003; breakeven inflation is the nominal yield minus the TIPS real yield at the same maturity.

Before 1990 the curve is rebuilt from the Federal Reserve's historical series on FRED. Before 1954 only monthly averages exist, and bill yields before 1982 are secondary-market rates quoted on a discount basis, so early curves are approximate.

U.S. Federal Debt and the Debt Ceiling

Loading…

Amounts in trillions of dollars. Source: Daily Treasury Statement via the Treasury Fiscal Data API. The red line is absent while the limit is suspended.

How to read this

The debt ceiling is the maximum amount of debt the Treasury may have outstanding, set by law and periodically raised or suspended by Congress. The room under the limit is the statutory limit minus the debt subject to it. The Treasury General Account (TGA) is the government's cash account at the Federal Reserve; adding it gives a rough measure of the money available before a debt-ceiling standoff:

funding available ≈ debt limit − debt subject to limit + TGA cash

The green line (debt minus cash) approaching the red line (the limit) shows that cushion shrinking. Treasuries mature every day, which frees room for new borrowing within the same limit. The chart does not show the Treasury's “extraordinary measures”, temporary accounting steps that can postpone hitting the limit.

Security types

  • Bills mature in under one year; notes in 2 to 10 years; bonds in 20 or 30 years.
  • Floating rate notes mature in two years and pay interest that resets with 13-week bill auctions.
  • TIPS are inflation-protected notes and bonds of any maturity up to 30 years.
  • Nonmarketable debt is not traded: mainly Government Account Series held by federal trust funds, plus savings bonds and State and Local Government Series.

Source: Monthly Statement of the Public Debt via the Treasury Fiscal Data API.

Rate Definitions & Official Data Sources

All values are stored and displayed in percentage units (e.g., 4.25 means 4.25%). Weekends and federal market holidays are never filled with synthetic values.

U.S. Treasury Par Yield Curve Rates

Published each business day by the U.S. Department of the Treasury. The par yield curve relates the par yield on a security to its time to maturity and is based on closing market bid yields on actively traded over-the-counter Treasury securities. Includes bills (1M, 6W, 2M, 3M, 4M, 6M) and notes/bonds (1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y, 20Y, 30Y).

  • Treasury Daily Par Yield Curve Table
  • Official Treasury XML Feed Documentation

Effective Federal Funds Rate (EFFR) & FOMC Target Range

Calculated and published each business day by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York as a volume-weighted median of overnight federal funds transactions. While the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) establishes the target range for the federal funds rate as a monetary policy stance, EFFR measures actual market transactions between depository institutions.

  • NY Fed EFFR Reference Rate Page
  • NY Fed Markets Data API Documentation

Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR)

Published each business day by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York in cooperation with the Office of Financial Research. SOFR is a broad measure of the cost of borrowing cash overnight collateralized by U.S. Treasury securities in the repurchase agreement (repo) market, and serves as the primary U.S. dollar benchmark reference rate.

  • NY Fed SOFR Reference Rate Page
  • NY Fed Data & API Terms of Use

Treasury yield curves

Daily par yield curve rates (1990–present) and par real yield curve rates for TIPS (2003–present) from the U.S. Department of the Treasury. Yields before 1990 come from the Federal Reserve Board's H.15 historical series on FRED: daily constant-maturity yields from 1962 (3- and 6-month bill rates from 1954 and 1958), monthly averages back to 1934.

  • Daily Treasury Par Yield Curve Rates
  • Daily Treasury Par Real Yield Curve Rates
  • Treasury yield curve methodology

Policy rates

Federal funds target range (DFEDTARL, DFEDTARU; DFEDTAR before December 2008), the effective federal funds rate (DFF) and the overnight reverse repo award rate (RRPONTSYAWARD), from FRED. EFFR and SOFR on the Rates Dashboard and in the Fed Policy card come directly from the New York Fed. FOMC meeting dates come from the Federal Reserve's meeting calendar; minutes are released three weeks after each decision.

  • FRED: Federal funds target range
  • New York Fed Markets Data API
  • FOMC meeting calendar

Inflation, GDP & jobs

CPI (CPIAUCNS, CPIAUCSL) and PPI final demand (PPIFID, PPIFIS) from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the PCE price index (PCEPI) and real GDP growth (A191RL1Q225SBEA) from the Bureau of Economic Analysis, and the unemployment rate (UNRATE). First releases and revisions come from the ALFRED vintage archive. Recessions: NBER dates (USREC). Release dates: the FRED release calendar.

  • FRED API documentation
  • ALFRED (archival FRED)

Federal debt

Debt subject to the limit, the statutory debt limit and the Treasury General Account balance from the Daily Treasury Statement; debt by security type from the Monthly Statement of the Public Debt — both through the Treasury Fiscal Data API.

  • Monthly Statement of the Public Debt
