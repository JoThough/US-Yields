Rate Definitions & Official Data Sources

All values are stored and displayed in percentage units (e.g., 4.25 means 4.25%). Weekends and federal market holidays are never filled with synthetic values.

U.S. Treasury Par Yield Curve Rates

Published each business day by the U.S. Department of the Treasury. The par yield curve relates the par yield on a security to its time to maturity and is based on closing market bid yields on actively traded over-the-counter Treasury securities. Includes bills (1M, 6W, 2M, 3M, 4M, 6M) and notes/bonds (1Y, 2Y, 3Y, 5Y, 7Y, 10Y, 20Y, 30Y).

  • Treasury Daily Par Yield Curve Table
  • Official Treasury XML Feed Documentation

Effective Federal Funds Rate (EFFR) & FOMC Target Range

Calculated and published each business day by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York as a volume-weighted median of overnight federal funds transactions. While the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) establishes the target range for the federal funds rate as a monetary policy stance, EFFR measures actual market transactions between depository institutions.

  • NY Fed EFFR Reference Rate Page
  • NY Fed Markets Data API Documentation

Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR)

Published each business day by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York in cooperation with the Office of Financial Research. SOFR is a broad measure of the cost of borrowing cash overnight collateralized by U.S. Treasury securities in the repurchase agreement (repo) market, and serves as the primary U.S. dollar benchmark reference rate.
